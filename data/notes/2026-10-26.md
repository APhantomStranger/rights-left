# When the World Says No to the Far Right

Last weekend gave us something we haven’t felt in a while: Tangible proof that the far right’s hold on power is not permanent. We’ve had “vibes” in our favor for the last few months, that *something* was changing. I (torture myself) participate in a few political groups online and the MAGA voices have been less obnoxious and cheerleading Trump for months now...However we haven’t had something we can point to and see a result of the push into Right Wing ideology slowing. In Brazil, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva came from behind in the runoff to beat Flávio Bolsonaro and win a record fourth term, with turnout above 80 percent. Flávio is the son of a jailed former president the Trump administration has openly defended. In Serbia, a student movement born out of protests over a train-station collapse that killed 16 people beat Aleksandar Vučić, the populist strongman who had dominated the country for more than a decade. Turnout there was the highest since the breakup of Yugoslavia.

In Israel, Benjamin Netanyahu’s coalition lost its majority in the Knesset, winning 53 of the 61 seats it needs, on the highest turnout in a generation. Add Hungary, where voters ended Viktor Orbán’s 16-year rule this spring, and Canada, where voters rallied behind Mark Carney last year instead of a Conservative campaign that borrowed from Trump’s playbook. A pattern is hard to miss. These aren’t identical stories, and the winners don’t all share one ideology. What they share is voters, especially young voters, turning out in huge numbers to reject leaders who treat government as their personal property. The Council on Foreign Relations put it plainly this week: reports of democracy’s demise “might be premature.” Trump’s fingerprints are on several of these races. In Brazil he backed the Bolsonaros, called Jair Bolsonaro’s prosecution a “witch hunt,” and hit the country with tariffs, and Reuters reported that State Department officials pushed for sanctions to weaken Lula. Brazilians answered at the ballot box.

All over the world, people are watching what Trump is doing to democracy here and deciding they don’t want it at home. If there is a silver lining to this presidency, it’s this. Trump has put a spotlight on right-wing values and shown everyone how easily they can be twisted into nationalism and dangerous ideas. “Law and order” became pardons for people who attacked the Capitol. “Patriotism” became loyalty to one man. “Protecting our borders” became masked agents and detention camps. For years these ideas hid behind friendlier language. Trump stripped the packaging off, and the rest of the world got a clear look at where it leads. Brazil, Serbia, Israel, Hungary and Canada have shown that the answer is to show up, vote, and refuse to normalize it. Our turn comes next week at the midterms.

— Chris
