# The Morning After the Wave

Before I get into this one... I want to admit something. The schadenfreude was real for me. On Tuesday night the country spoke and it said there's going to be some bad things happening to bad people, one of my favorite passtimes to witness.

## The night, in brief

Democrats took back the Senate, flipping seven Republican-held seats, from Maine to Kansas to Texas, Iowa sealed it, and Kansas sent a Democrat to the Senate for the first time since 1932.

As well as this stunning night for women!

**Keisha Lance Bottoms** is the next governor of Georgia: the first woman, and the first Black person, ever elected to lead that state. Georgia. I did not hear about this until Tuesday night and  had my fingers crossed the entire time.

**Peggy Flanagan** of Minnesota becomes the first Native American woman in the U.S. Senate. **Annie Andrews** is the first woman South Carolina has ever elected to the Senate. **Deb Haaland** is the first Native American woman elected governor anywhere, in New Mexico. **Amy Acton** is Ohio's first woman governor. **Gina Hinojosa** is Texas's first Hispanic governor. And somewhere in Colorado, **Melat Kiros** is about to become the first Gen Z woman in Congress.

## The weeks before

Just in general, it's so cathartic... The Rallies. Marches. Signs. Standing with 50501 and No Kings, shouting into what felt a lot like a void. It wasn't for nothing. I said it before on many of my posts, this was our Democracies last chance to ring the bell and sound the alarm. I truly felt, and still do, that if we would have remained silent, it would spell the end of America as we knew it.

And the whole time, I heard it. *You're overreacting.* *Calm down.* *It's not that serious.* 
Here's the thing about being called dramatic, Maybe you ARE the guy yelling at clouds. Maybe nobody else sees what you see, or worse...they see it and don't care. And then last night happened, and America showed up.

## Now... the part where I pump the brakes

Let me be clear. Trump is not out of power. Not yet. He's still in the White House, and the people he put in place are still in their seats. A new Congress doesn't get sworn in until January, and a lot can happen between now and then.

## Accountability, not revenge
We cannot let a usurper song-and-dance his way into our government, install his own crew of cronies to do his bidding, and treat Congress and every other seat of power like they're props in his show. That's what we were voting against. That's what this Congress has to actually stop.
And I want to say this plainly, I'm not looking for vengeance. I don't want a mirror image of what we just lived through, with the names swapped. BUT. We need to show anyone in the future this is not going to happen again. And to make example of those who tried to remake the country on their whims.

And gee golly I want accountability.

We didn't go through the last two years... the last ten years... for nothing. People broke laws. People abused power. People hurt our neighbors and called it policy. That ledger is real, Those people need to answer for it. Investigations. Hearings. Courts. The boring, slow, constitutional machinery that was built for exactly this.

No revenge. Just consequences. There's a difference, and it matters.

Oh... and while we're at it?

**Abolish ICE.**

— Chris
